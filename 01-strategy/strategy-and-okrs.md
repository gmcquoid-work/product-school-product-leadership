# Product Strategy & OKRs: Meridian Foundations

> Module 1 · Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration** | The Meridian project management platform is useful for both office and field teams and pulls the full workflow for projects into one easy-to-use product. |
| **Where to play** | Construction contractors running complex projects, providing administrative power and frontline usefulness |
| **How to win** | Experience with complex projects being run by 38 of the top 100 us construction firms |
| **Capabilities required** | Supporting the complex needs of our office as well as frontline teams |
| **Management systems** | Weekly and monthly usage metrics by cohort to see if adoption for field teams is increasing |

## 2. Your one hard no

> **Hard no:** We will not pursue new top-10 General Contractor logos or build new analytics or ERP integrations this year. Enterprise accounts we already serve will be field pilots, not feature sponsors.

## 3. OKR cascade

> **Objective:** Make Meridian the daily system field teams use by turning job site photos, status updates, RFIs and issue capture into the natural starting point for every project workflow.
>
> - **KR1:** Increase the share of site issues, status updates, and RFIs that originate from field capture in Meridian by superintendents and foremen from 6% to 40%.
> - **KR2:** Field-raised issues get a visible response or action from the office within 24 hours in 80% of cases.
> - **KR3:** Achieve a PSAT of 4.0+ average monthly rating from field users on the ease of capturing photos, RFI's and updating status on-site. Whilst maintaining or improving the existing enterprise PSAT scores.

## 4. AI pressure-test

**Most valid challenge:** Firstly, it challenged my hard no, which was not to go after the top 10 enterprise contractors, but the wording around continuing to add analytics and ERP integrations that would support field adoption is a loophole, and any enterprise feature request could be argued into it, which is valid. The recommendation was to tighten it or admit it wasn't a hard no.  Secondly, it challenged the claim that it was a usability problem, which is the focus of my key results. Whilst usability could be a factor, WhatsApp is a coordination tool. Allowing greater visibility and quick answers would help the behaviour we want to change for Meridian, with the starting point being the Meridian team.  Thirdly, there was a challenge in measuring the existing enterprise PSAT, suggesting it could serve as a guardrail rather than a Key Result.

**What I will change and what I will defend:** I'm going to tighten up the language of my hard no, which makes it easier to defend: We will not pursue new top-10 General Contractor logos or build new analytics or ERP integrations this year. Enterprise accounts we already serve will be field pilots, not feature sponsors. I am also going to change one of my key results to focus on getting a responsible action, so the key result measures the value exchange by the field, getting faster resolution than they would from a chat, or at least a result that is trackable and measurable.  I'm going to defend the 3rd KR, as we need to ensure we're measuring our existing PSAT to see if our improvements are making an impact. Also, if this changes significantly, we can course-correct or review the strategy.
